# Bioenergy

Control use of trees, waste, agricultural crops for energy. Feedstocks (wood, waste, crops, algae) burned as solids, liquids, or gases. Some sustainable; some exceed coal's carbon impact. BECCS theoretical but unproven at scale.

## Examples
**Discouraging**: phasing out infra investment, raising costs, public campaigns on unsustainable sources.
**Encouraging**: incentives for feedstock cropland, R&D for biofuels/vehicles, GHG accounting exemptions.

## Big Messages
- "Bioenergy is not a high leverage response to climate change"
- Subsidizing wood bioenergy increases temperature (higher net emissions)

## Key Dynamics
- Temperature barely changes; supply-constrained
- Encouraging wood raises gross CO2 and reduces removals
- True zero-carbon requires regrowth — slow for trees
- BECCS requires full biomass regen + capture (unproven)

## Co-Benefits of Discouraging
- Frees cropland for food
- Preserves forest biodiversity
- Better air quality

## Equity
- Bioenergy crops reduce food production land
- Market shifts impact farmers — need transition support

## Slider Settings
| Level | Range |
|-------|-------|
| Highly discouraged | +200% to +100% |
| Discouraged | +100% to 0% |
| Less encouraged | 0% to -10% |
| Status quo | -10% to -20% |
| More encouraged | -20% to -50% |

Current global bioenergy ~15% subsidies.

## Model Structure
Feedstocks: wood / crops / waste-other with separate tax/subsidy. Wood produces net emissions; others regenerate faster. Encouragement drives deforestation.

## FAQs
CCS via Coal/Gas/Bioenergy advanced views, carbon pricing, or clean electricity standards (with caveats).
