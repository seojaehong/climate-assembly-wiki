# Coal

**Discourage or encourage mining coal and burning it in power plants.** Coal is the most harmful fossil fuel for carbon emissions and air pollutants. Cheap to extract; carbon capture and storage (CCS) limited.

## Examples
- Government policies phasing out plants or raising costs through subsidy removal/taxation
- Financial institutions limiting capital for new coal infrastructure

## Big Messages
- Discouraging coal provides high-leverage temperature reduction — "coal emits more carbon dioxide when it is burned than either oil or natural gas"
- Air quality improvements reduce medical costs

## Key Dynamics
- **Impact**: Coal consumption visibly decreases
- **"Squeezing the Balloon"**: Gas demand rises when coal is discouraged unless gas restrictions or carbon pricing exist
- **Price-Demand Feedback**: Higher prices push efficiency but burden low-income households

## Co-Benefits
- Reduced air pollution → better health
- Less mining → less heavy-metal water contamination

## Equity
- Coal taxation raises costs for dependent households/businesses
- Coal-region workers need alternative employment

## Slider Settings
- Highly discouraged: +200% to +100%
- Status quo: ~30% subsidy
- More encouraged: -35% to -50%

## Model Structure
Coal costs influence new capacity investment, existing plant utilization, and retirement timing (~30-year average).

## Case Studies
- US: replacing coal with solar could save ~52,000 lives/yr
- US total coal cost: $344B/yr (incl. $187B air pollution)
- India: 1 GW coal capacity raises infant mortality ~15% nearby

## FAQs
Reduce coal via advanced sliders (new infra, utilization, accelerated retirement). CCS via subsidies, carbon pricing, or clean electricity standards.
