# En-ROADS Dynamics

## Complex Interactions Between Energy Supplies and Demand

### 1. Delays and Capital Stock Turnover
Only ~6% of global energy infrastructure replaced annually; power plants and refineries last 30+ years. Vehicles (10–15 yr) turn over faster but still slowly.

### 2. Price and Demand Effects
Higher energy prices reduce demand; subsidizing renewables lowers overall energy prices and increases demand — partially undermining climate benefits.

### 3. Competition Between Energy Sources ("Crowding Out", "Squeezing the Balloon")
"The contributions of renewables, nuclear, and new zero-carbon sources are not additive — more of one means less of another."

### 4. Complementary Policies: Electrification
Clean electricity only helps when buildings, industry, transport can use it. Pair renewables with electrification.

### 5. Economies of Scale and Learning
Renewable costs decline ~20% per doubling of capacity (progress ratio 0.80 vs coal 0.98).

### 6. Economic Damage from Climate Change
Rising temperatures reduce GDP via crop failure, disasters, biodiversity loss — lower GDP reduces emissions (self-limiting feedback).

## Drivers of the Baseline Scenario

### Growth
Global GDP growth overwhelms efficiency and fuel-mix improvements.

### Non-CO2 Emissions
Methane, N2O, F-gases ~26% of GHG. Agriculture, waste, industry matter.

## System Dynamics of the Climate

### Bathtub Dynamics
Atmospheric CO2 rises when emissions exceed removals. Stabilization needs emissions = removals.

### Delays
Temperature continues rising after CO2 stabilizes due to ocean thermal inertia (equilibration beyond 2100).
