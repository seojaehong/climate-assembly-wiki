# Renewables

Strategies for encouraging solar, geothermal, wind. Excludes nuclear and bioenergy.

## Examples
Government tax incentives for residential solar, landowner agreements for wind, R&D investment, corporate 100% renewable commitments.

## Main Takeaways
1. Renewable subsidies help limit fossil demand; renewables become cheapest electricity over time.
2. Maximum impact when paired with electrification.
3. High penetration requires storage solutions.

## Key Dynamics
- **Price-Demand Feedback**: lower costs → more consumption (partially offsets benefits).
- **Economies of Scale**: baseline already captures past gains; breakthroughs needed for more.
- **Delays**: subsidies phase in over 10 years.
- **Complementary**: "Incentivizing electrification of buildings & industry and transport enables electricity from renewables to replace fuel" like oil.

## Co-Benefits
- Cleaner air/water → better health
- Energy access & reliability
- Jobs across skill levels

## Equity
Cost barriers persist for low-income communities; many subsidies favor homeowners.

## Slider Settings
+50% tax to -100% subsidy. Status quo ~-25% subsidy.

## Limitations
Renewables alone cannot avoid significant warming — may just meet new demand. Pair with fossil discouragement + electrification.
