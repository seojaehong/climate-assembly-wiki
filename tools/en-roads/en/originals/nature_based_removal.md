# Nature-Based Carbon Dioxide Removal

Afforestation, reforestation, agricultural soil carbon sequestration, biochar — store C in plants/soils. Storage is reversible (fire, conversion, decay).

## Methods
- **Afforestation/Reforestation**: trees absorb CO2 during growth
- **Agricultural soil sequestration**: no-till, prevent overgrazing
- **Biochar**: pyrolyzed biomass buried for long-term storage

## Key Messages
- Can remove meaningful CO2 but limited
- Storage reversible — fire, pests, harvest, management
- Modest scale relative to fossil emissions

## Important Dynamics
- **Delay**: new forests take decades
- **Reversibility**: fire/pests release stored C
- **Land**: substantial acreage required
- **Scale**: can't offset energy emissions alone

## Co-Benefits
Biodiversity, urban cooling, jobs, farmer income (carbon markets), soil health.

## Equity
- Monocultures < natural forests for biodiversity
- Large land shifts can harm Indigenous and low-income communities
- Biochar feedstock may compete with food/habitat

## Slider Settings
Adjusts three methods simultaneously, 0% to +100% of maximum potential. Afforestation uses up to 550 million hectares of available land.
